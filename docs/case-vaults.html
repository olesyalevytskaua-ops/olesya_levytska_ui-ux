Vaults1 · Product Design Case Study

Vaults1

A clearer, more confident path from search to reservation for a premium self-storage platform.

PROJECT OVERVIEW

Role
Senior Product Designer
Platforms
Responsive website
Industry
Self-storage, online booking

Vaults1 is a premium self-storage platform that enables customers to search, compare, and reserve secure storage units online.

The project combined marketing pages, informational content, an online booking experience, pricing calculations, payment flow, and customer account functionality into one seamless responsive platform.

MY ROLE

As Senior Product Designer, I focused on simplifying the booking process while maximising conversion and user confidence throughout the customer journey.

The situation

A long-running project that needed a way to close

When I joined Computools, Vaults1 had already been in progress for a long time. It had run well past its agreed hours, and the relationship between the client and the team was tense.

Vaults1 was rebranding and expanding beyond Latvia and Lithuania, opening locations in Italy, Spain and France, with Poland planned. The existing website had clearly been assembled by developers without a design process: it looked cheap and could not carry a premium brand into new markets.

Our scope was design only. Vaults1 runs bank-grade, certified storage, so the build stays with its own approved development team. We delivered the design and handed it off to them.

PROCESS & CONTRIBUTION

From stalled to signed off.

1. The real problem

Customers couldn’t picture a size in centimetres

The client’s phone lines kept getting the same question: which box do I need? Sizes were listed only as dimensions, and 30 × 43 × 5 cm means little when you are holding documents, a watch and some cash. Unsure customers called, hesitated or put off booking.

2. Showing what fits

A box you can see inside

Instead of adding more copy, each size became a transparent box filled with familiar valuables: documents and a flash drive in XXS, jewellery and a watch in S, gold bars and a gift box in L and XL, a framed painting in XXXL. The dimensions stay on the box, so the picture and the numbers confirm each other.

Prices sit beside each size by rental term (1–5, 6–11 and 12+ months), so the saving on a longer term is visible at the moment of choice. The aim was fewer calls and faster, more confident decisions.

3. Rebrand

One premium look for every new market

Working from the client’s new brand book (black and white, a single yellow accent, bold uppercase headlines), I redesigned the whole site: home, pricing, FAQ, news, rules and the booking flow, plus a set of stylish campaign banners.

The structure was built to grow, so new countries and locations could be added without redesigning the site.

4. Faster booking

Booking from the first screen

Right under the header sits a quick-booking bar, like the search on Booking.com or an airline site: location, box size, start and end dates, and one button. It takes people straight to a matching box, so booking is closer, easier to use and easier to understand than a path through several pages.

Alongside it, I fixed the details that slow people down at the moment of paying: how visible the buttons are, where they sit, and clear feedback when a button is pressed during checkout.

5. Sign-off

Getting a demanding client to yes

The hardest part was not a screen. Both sides had been stuck for months. We found common ground on the style and then on every screen, and reached sign-off with a client who had been very hard to satisfy. The approved design was handed off to their certified development team.

The redesigned site

Light, dark, and the mix the client chose

I explored a fully light and a fully dark version of the site. The client chose a third direction: dark and light blocks alternating down the page, so each section stands clearly apart.

The booking bar sits in the first screen, the three-step “how it works” explains the service before the price, and sizes appear as filled boxes in a carousel. The same layout holds on mobile. Select a screen to see the full page.
