Vaults1 · Product Design Case Study

Vaults1

A clearer, more confident path from search to reservation for a premium self-storage platform.

PROJECT OVERVIEW

Role
Senior Product & UX/UI Designer
Platforms
Responsive website
Industry
Self-storage, online booking

Vaults1 is a premium self-storage platform that enables customers to search, compare, and reserve secure storage units online.

The project combined marketing pages, informational content, an online booking experience, pricing calculations, payment flow, and customer account functionality into one seamless responsive platform.

MY ROLE

I owned the design: discovery, the size visualisation, the quick-booking bar and checkout, and the redesign of every page, through to client sign-off.

The brand book came from the client. The build stays with Vaults1’s own certified development team, so my scope ended at design handoff.

The situation

A long-running project that needed a way to close

When I joined Computools, Vaults1 had already been in progress for a long time. It had run well past its agreed hours, and the relationship between the client and the team was tense.

STARTING POINT

  • A rebrand for expansion beyond Latvia and Lithuania: Italy, Spain and France, with Poland planned
  • An existing site built by developers without a design process, which looked cheap for a premium brand
  • A project over its agreed hours, with tension on both sides
  • Design-only scope: Vaults1 runs bank-grade, certified storage, so the build stays with its own approved team

PROCESS & CONTRIBUTION

From stalled to signed off.

1. Discovery

Where customers hesitate

The primary objective was to reduce friction throughout the booking experience while making complex pricing and storage options easy to understand.

The booking process included multiple decision points, which made information hierarchy and progressive disclosure essential for a smooth experience.

ACTIVITIES

  • UX research
  • User journey mapping
  • Competitive analysis
  • Information architecture
  • Customer flow mapping
  • Content structure
  • Responsive strategy

The key finding: sizes in centimetres don’t help

The client’s phone lines kept getting the same question: which box do I need? Sizes were listed only as dimensions, and 30 × 43 × 5 cm means little when you are holding documents, a watch and some cash. Unsure customers called, hesitated or put off booking.

2. Showing what fits

A box you can see inside

Instead of adding more copy, each size became a transparent box filled with familiar valuables: documents and a flash drive in XXS, jewellery and a watch in S, gold bars and a gift box in L and XL, a framed painting in XXXL. The dimensions stay on the box, so the picture and the numbers confirm each other.

Prices sit beside each size by rental term (1–5, 6–11 and 12+ months), so the saving on a longer term is visible at the moment of choice. The aim was fewer calls and faster, more confident decisions.

3. Rebrand

One premium look for every new market

Working from the client’s new brand book (black and white, a single yellow accent, bold uppercase headlines), I redesigned the whole site.

REDESIGNED

  • Home page
  • Pricing
  • FAQ
  • News
  • Rules
  • Booking flow
  • Campaign banners

The structure was built to grow, so new countries and locations could be added without redesigning the site.

4. Faster booking

Booking from the first screen

Right under the header sits a quick-booking bar, like the search on Booking.com or an airline site. It takes people straight to a matching box, so booking is closer, easier to use and easier to understand than a path through several pages.

QUICK-BOOKING BAR

  • Location
  • Box size
  • Start and end dates
  • One button to a matching box

CHECKOUT FIXES

  • More visible buttons
  • Buttons where people expect them
  • Clear feedback when a button is pressed

5. Sign-off

Getting a demanding client to yes

The hardest part was not a screen. Both sides had been stuck for months. We found common ground on the style and then on every screen, and reached sign-off with a client who had been very hard to satisfy.

DELIVERED

  • An approved visual style
  • Every screen signed off
  • Design handoff to the client’s certified development team

HOW SUCCESS IS MEASURED

The build sits with the client’s team, so success shows up in their numbers:

  • Fewer calls asking which box size to choose
  • Share of bookings started from the quick-booking bar
  • Booking completion, from size choice to payment
  • Share of bookings on 6+ month terms

The redesigned site

Light, dark, and the mix the client chose

I explored a fully light and a fully dark version of the site. The client chose a third direction: dark and light blocks alternating down the page, so each section stands clearly apart.

The booking bar sits in the first screen, the three-step “how it works” explains the service before the price, and sizes appear as filled boxes in a carousel. The same layout holds on mobile. Select a screen to see the full page.
